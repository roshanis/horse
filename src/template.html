    Houses and people

    Why the kingdom kept falling apart, who was who, and how the Venad line was kept alive by adoption. Each person links to the events that name them.

    Trippappur house

    Rulers of Venad, with the women's palace at Attingal. Kept dying out and adopting from Kolathiri in the north (1681, 1696, 1718). Martanda Varma's line. pp. 2-4, 186; table 3

    Jayasimhanad house

    Princes of Quilon, the Dutch 'Desinganadu', with the women's palace at Kallada. From 1722 ruled by princes adopted from Kayamkulam, so in practice one block with the Cheraway house. pp. 36-37; table 5

    Cheraway house

    Odanad, that is Kayamkulam, and Karunagappalli. Claimed seniority over all three branches and fought Travancore for Karunagappalli and Nedumangadu for forty years. pp. 2, 16-25; tables 6-8

    Perakat branch of Nedumangadu

    The hill principality around Kottarakkara, the 'pepper-storeroom of Malabar'. Its princes kept claiming Venad by adoption; annexed in 1736. pp. 11-12, 65; table 4

    Pillamar and madumbimar

    Temple administrators in Venad and hereditary officials in the smaller states. They collected the taxes with their nayar militias, so every claimant needed them, and they changed sides whenever one grew strong. pp. 5-11, 185-187

    The northern states

    Purakkad, Tekkumkur and Vadakkumkur, nominally under the Cochin king, the head of the chovvaram Brahmin faction. Divided by their own successions and taken one by one after 1745. pp. 119-147, 190

    The Venad line by adoption

    Simplified from the book's genealogical table 3 (p. 230). Marumakkathayam: the heir is the sister's son, so every adoption brings in women as well as men.
